**Elevated plasma lipopolysaccharide concentrations in pulmonary tuberculosis associate with immunometabolic outcomes**

**Background:** Regulation of the host inflammatory response during tuberculosis (TB) disease remains incompletely understood. Microbial translocation, characterized by the movement of microorganisms and their products such as lipopolysaccharide (LPS) into systemic circulation, is a potential driver of immune activation in infectious diseases. We aimed to evaluate whether people with TB disease experience increased microbial translocation at TB diagnosis and whether this was associated with immune activation and glucose regulation. 

**Methods:** We conducted a 6-month prospective cohort study in the country of Georgia among HIV-negative adults (≥18 years) with microbiologically confirmed pulmonary TB (PTB). Household contacts without TB disease were control participants. Microbial translocation was assessed by measuring plasma concentrations of LPS at TB diagnosis and treatment completion. Plasma concentrations of 37 cytokines were profiled, and participants were assessed for stress hyperglycemia using repeat measures of fasting blood glucose and glycated hemoglobin during TB treatment.

**Findings:** Participants with PTB (n=80) had median age 37, were 57.5% male, and 43% (n=35) had stress hyperglycemia, while control participants (n=75) had median age 41 and were 36% male. Compared to controls, participants with PTB had significantly elevated plasma concentrations of LPS (median 0.47 vs 0.10 EU/ml (Endotoxin Units per milliliter), p<0.0001) at diagnosis. Euglycemic TB participants had significantly elevated LPS concentrations vs. those with TB–stress hyperglycemia (median 1.47 vs 0.23 EU/mL, p<0.0001). At the end of TB treatment, while LPS levels in participants with TB–euglycemia were similar to the control group, LPS remained significantly elevated in participants with TB–stress hyperglycemia (median 0.24 vs 0.10 among controls EU/mL, p<0.0001). Increased LPS at TB diagnosis was positively correlated with IL-23 (ρ=0.45, p=0.001) and negatively correlated with IL-17A (ρ=-0.34, p=0.018), with distinct cytokine correlation patterns according to glycemic status.

**Interpretation:** These data show PTB may cause increased microbial translocation that persists after TB cure in patients with stress hyperglycemia.
